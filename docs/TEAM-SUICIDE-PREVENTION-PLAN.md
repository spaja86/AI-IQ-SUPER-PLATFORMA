# Team Suicide-Prevention Plan

> This document defines a practical team-level prevention and response protocol for suicide risk, emotional crisis, and severe burnout concerns.
>
> If someone may be in immediate danger, contact local emergency services immediately.

## 1) Crisis protocol

- Define warning signs relevant to the workplace (hopelessness, severe withdrawal, alarming messages, talk of self-harm).
- Define escalation levels:
  - **Level 1:** concern, no immediate danger.
  - **Level 2:** urgent concern, same-day intervention required.
  - **Level 3:** immediate danger, emergency services required.
- Keep one shared and controlled emergency contact list:
  - Local emergency number(s)
  - National crisis hotline(s)
  - Internal emergency contacts (HR + duty manager)

## 2) Trained responders and ownership

- Assign a minimum response group:
  - HR lead
  - Direct manager backup
  - Mental-health first-aid trained contact(s)
- Define on-call rotation for urgent events during workdays.
- Keep role responsibilities explicit:
  - Who receives reports
  - Who performs first outreach
  - Who documents and closes incidents

## 3) Confidential reporting channels

- Provide at least two channels:
  - Anonymous form
  - Direct private contact (email/phone/DM) to response group
- Publish confidentiality rules and limits (including duty-to-protect exceptions).
- Guarantee non-retaliation for people who raise concerns in good faith.

## 4) Professional support partnerships

- Maintain referral paths with licensed mental-health providers.
- Maintain local crisis hotline and emergency routing information by region.
- Predefine referral handoff steps (who contacts, when, and what minimum information is shared).

## 5) Team-lead training

- Run recurring training for leads on:
  - Recognizing warning signs
  - Supportive and non-judgmental communication
  - Escalation boundaries and when to call emergency services
- Include scenario-based drills at least twice per year.

## 6) Preventive check-ins and burnout controls

- Run regular 1:1 check-ins with explicit wellbeing prompts.
- Track workload pressure signals (overtime, repeated incidents, staffing imbalance).
- Apply preventive actions quickly (workload reduction, time off, temporary role adjustments).

## 7) Post-incident follow-up and return-to-work

- Record incident timeline, actions taken, and responsible owners.
- Define post-incident follow-up windows (24h, 72h, 2 weeks, 1 month).
- Prepare return-to-work support plans where applicable:
  - Reduced load
  - Scheduled check-ins
  - Clear accommodation points

## 8) Quarterly review and continuous improvement

- Review incident metrics and response quality every quarter.
- Update:
  - Emergency contacts
  - Partner referrals
  - Training completion and gaps
- Run retrospective actions with documented owners and due dates.

## Operational checklist

- [ ] Crisis levels and escalation path documented
- [ ] Emergency contacts verified
- [ ] Response group assigned and trained
- [ ] Anonymous and direct reporting channels active
- [ ] Professional referral network confirmed
- [ ] Team-lead training schedule active
- [ ] Burnout prevention routine in place
- [ ] Post-incident follow-up template adopted
- [ ] Quarterly review cadence scheduled
